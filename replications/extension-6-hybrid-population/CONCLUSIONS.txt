Why a few AI agents on the hubs destroy cooperation
===================================================
Finding from hybrid_experiment.py (see figures/hybrid_population.png):
at 10% AI, random placement leaves 0.67 cooperation, hub placement only
0.08. Humans end up cooperating less than the AI agents. Checked by
instrumenting one run (seed 1, 10% AI, 360 generations), not assumed.

WHAT IT SHOWS
  1. Reach. 50 AI hubs touch 364 of the 450 humans directly. 50 random AI
     agents touch only 135.
  2. AI hubs are the best-paid players (2.15 vs. 0.67 for humans), because
     a hub collects a payoff from every link. Humans imitate whoever earns
     more, so they copy the AI hubs, and the AI hubs mostly defect: in a PD
     defection pays more per game against any fixed set of neighbours, and
     that is all a Q-learner sees.
  3. The Santos mechanism breaks. On a scale-free network a defecting hub
     loses payoff once its leaves defect too, and then imitates a
     cooperating neighbour back: the hub is retaken. An AI hub never
     imitates, so it is never retaken. It keeps defecting at the rate its
     own Q values dictate, and keeps being copied.
  4. Why humans cooperate less than AI: AI agents still explore (softmax
     picks C about 1 time in 4), while imitating humans lock onto
     defection once every well-paid neighbour defects.
  With random placement the same effect is local: humans next to an AI
  cooperate 0.30, the rest 0.51.

TAKEAWAY
  In this model, the paper's "hubs anchor cooperation" result needs the
  hubs to imitate. Put learners that don't imitate on the hubs, and a small
  share of them is enough to remove cooperation from the whole network.


CONTROL: IS IT Q-LEARNING? (control_experiment.py, tests/test_08)
  At 10% AI on the hubs, final cooperation:
    Q-learning 0.084 | coin flip 0.083 | always D 0.007 | always C 0.994
  (no AI: 0.899). Coin-flip hubs ignore payoffs entirely and do the same
  damage as Q-learning hubs. So the collapse needs only hubs that never
  imitate. The hubs are a lever: humans end up copying whatever the hubs
  play. At random placement every rule stays between 0.68 and 0.91.
  Side note: always-C AI placed at random slightly LOWERS cooperation at
  5% (0.852 < 0.899) -- unconditional cooperators feed their defecting
  neighbours, who then look worth copying. Not instrumented yet.

ROBUSTNESS: TAU (tau_sweep.py, tests/test_09)
  Hub collapse holds for tau >= 0.1 (0.05-0.08). At tau 0.01 it weakens to
  0.50, because greedy learners freeze on their random first move.
  Diagnostic (seed 1, 10% AI on hubs, 360 generations):

python3 -c "
import random, sys
sys.path.insert(0, '../..')
from network import build_network
from payoff import compute_payoffs
from update_rule import next_strategy
from q_learning_rule import softmax_action, update_q
from main_hybrid import assign_ai
R, S, T, P = 1, -0.1, 1.2, 0
for tau in [0.01, 0.1]:
    rng = random.Random(1)
    g = build_network('scale_free', 500, seed=1, m=2)
    ai = assign_ai(g, 0.1, 'hubs', rng)
    strat = {v: ('C' if rng.random() < 0.5 else 'D') for v in g.nodes}
    first = {v: strat[v] for v in ai}
    q = {v: {'C': 0.0, 'D': 0.0} for v in ai}
    switches = {v: 0 for v in ai}
    for gen in range(360):
        pay = compute_payoffs(g, strat, R, S, T, P)
        new = {}
        for v in g.nodes:
            if v in ai:
                q[v] = update_q(q[v], strat[v], pay[v] / g.degree[v], 0.1)
                new[v] = softmax_action(q[v], tau, rng)
                switches[v] += new[v] != strat[v]
            else:
                new[v] = next_strategy(g, v, strat, pay, T, S, rng)
        strat = new
    same = sum(strat[v] == first[v] for v in ai)
    never = sum(switches[v] == 0 for v in ai)
    print(f'tau {tau}: AI still playing their first action: {same}/50 | never switched: {never}/50 | AI C {sum(strat[v]==\"C\" for v in ai)/50:.2f} | all C {sum(s==\"C\" for s in strat.values())/500:.2f}')

Expected output:
tau 0.01: AI still playing their first action: 50/50 | never switched: 49/50 | AI C 0.52 | all C 0.60
tau 0.1: AI still playing their first action: 28/50 | never switched: 0/50 | AI C 0.36 | all C 0.09

  Why they freeze: both Q values start at 0. Whatever a hub plays first
  earns a positive reward (either action pays > 0 per game when about half
  the neighbours cooperate), so that action's Q rises above the untried
  one. With tau 0.01 a gap of 0.05 already gives p > 0.99, so the other
  action is never tried again. With tau 0.1 hubs keep exploring, find that
  D pays more, and drift to defection.

UPDATED TAKEAWAY
  Hubs that never imitate set the network's level of cooperation, whatever
  drives them. Q-learning is one such driver; its details (tau) decide
  which way the hubs lean, not whether they decide.
