FINDING 1 -- the prediction holds: code-reading replaces the network
  Cooperation is 0.97-1.00 on every network, over the whole grid AND in
  the PD corner, where plain imitation is 0.000 everywhere at this scale.
  DefectBot is extinct in every run average (0.000 on every network).
  FairBot + PrudentBot hold 91-100% of agents in the PD corner (100% on
  complete, 91% on scale_free -- the rest is mostly CliqueBot).

FINDING 2 -- the topology ordering is gone, and slightly REVERSED
  Plain imitation: complete < single_scale < scale_free_random <
  scale_free (0.49 -> 0.65). Open-source: complete 0.995 > single_scale
  0.987 > scale_free_random 0.980 > scale_free 0.974. The gap is small
  (0.02), but it runs the other way, and in the same order on both
  measures.

FINDING 3 -- heterogeneity keeps CliqueBot alive
  CliqueBot share rises steadily with heterogeneity: 0.016 (complete) ->
  0.021 -> 0.087 -> 0.143 (scale_free). CliqueBot vs FairBot/PrudentBot is
  mutual D, so every surviving CliqueBot cluster that borders the
  FairBot/PrudentBot majority adds D actions. That is a plausible cause of
  the small reversal in Finding 2 (hypothesis, NOT tested).

FINDING 4 -- CooperateBot survives only outside the PD corner
  Whole grid: 12-15% CooperateBot. PD corner: ~0. Where T is high,
  PrudentBot's exploitation of CooperateBot pays enough for it to spread;
  elsewhere a CooperateBot next to FairBots earns the same as they do and
  imitation never removes it (neutral drift, no mutation).

CAVEATS
  - The outcome table is fixed and cost-free: code-reading programs get
    their advantage for nothing. The proof-cost experiment (next, per
    ARCHITECTURE.txt) is where the network effect could come back.
  - Reduced scale, 10 realizations. The 0.02 reversal is consistent in
    direction but small; not tested for significance.
  - Random 1/5-each start: code-readers begin at 60% of the population.
    Whether they can INVADE from a small minority is the separate
    follow-up already planned.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PhD FRAMING
  With transparency, cooperation no longer needs network structure: every
  network reaches near-full cooperation, even in the Prisoner's Dilemma.
  The heterogeneous networks that help plain imitators instead shelter a
  brittle "copy-recognising" program (CliqueBot) that doesn't cooperate
  with the robust ones. Relevant to Critch et al.'s Open Problem 2: in
  this setting, a population containing FairBots (unbounded DUPOCs) does
  converge to mostly agents that reward legible cooperation -- but only
  from a start where they're already common, and with free proofs.
