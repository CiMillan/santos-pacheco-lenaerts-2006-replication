Extension 7 -- conclusions

1. Which beta maximizes cooperation (all human)?
   beta = 0.56. Mean 0.974 (10 runs), 0.985 (50 runs). Above it a slight
   dip, 0.945 at beta 10. Same shape as Pinheiro, Santos & Pacheco (2012),
   Fig. 4, but weak: 48 vs 43 of 50 runs end all-C (peak vs beta 10).
   Source: tests/test_03_beta_sweep.txt, tests/test_04_peak_check.txt.

2. Low beta is drift.
   Below beta 0.1, payoffs barely matter. Runs end all-C, all-D or mixed by
   chance (tests/test_02_run_fermi.txt, ten seeds at beta 0), so the
   10-run means (0.24-0.51) are noise, not a trend.

3. Fermi optimum vs the paper's rule.
   0.985 at beta 0.56 vs 0.90 for the Santos rule (extension 6, 0% AI).

4. With 10% AI agents on the hubs, no beta helps.
   Cooperation falls monotonically, 0.30 at beta 0.01 to 0.06 at beta 10.
   Hub cooperation stays near 0.30 throughout (the AI agents' own mix).
   Higher beta = humans copy the best-paid players more reliably, and
   those are the mostly-defecting AI hubs.

5. Reading (my framing, no source).
   Pinheiro et al. explain the optimum by hubs being preferred role models.
   That helps only when the hubs are imitators themselves. Hubs that never
   imitate turn the same mechanism against cooperation.
