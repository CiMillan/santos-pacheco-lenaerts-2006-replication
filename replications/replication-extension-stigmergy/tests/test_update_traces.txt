cd ~/Desktop/cooperation-atlas/replications/santos-pacheco-lenaerts-2006/replication-extension-stigmergy
python3 -c "
from stigmergy_update_rule import update_traces
traces = {0: 0.0, 1: 4.0}
payoffs = {0: 2.0, 1: 0.0}
print('lam=1  :', update_traces(traces, payoffs, 1))
print('lam=0.5:', update_traces(traces, payoffs, 0.5))
t = {0: 0.0}
for _ in range(10):
    t = update_traces(t, {0: 2.0}, 0.2)
print('lam=0.2, 10 steps of payoff 2:', round(t[0], 3), '(expect 2*(1-0.8**10) =', round(2*(1-0.8**10), 3), ')')
"

Expected output (exact -- no randomness here):
lam=1  : {0: 2.0, 1: 0.0}
lam=0.5: {0: 1.0, 1: 2.0}
lam=0.2, 10 steps of payoff 2: 1.785 (expect 2*(1-0.8**10) = 1.785 )

Why: with lam=1 the old trace is forgotten completely, so each trace is just
this generation's payoff -- that is the paper's rule, and the reason lam=1 is
the built-in check. With lam=0.5 each trace lands halfway between old and new
(0 and 2 -> 1; 4 and 0 -> 2). With lam=0.2 and a constant payoff of 2, the
trace creeps up toward 2 but only gets 1 - 0.8^10 = 89% of the way after 10
generations: small lam = long memory. A bug would show up as a trace that
overshoots the payoff, or that doesn't change at lam=1.
